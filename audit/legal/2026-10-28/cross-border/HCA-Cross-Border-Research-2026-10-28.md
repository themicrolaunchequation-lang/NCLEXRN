## 1. UK: which geographical address?

**Short answer:** Where the CCR duties apply, Schedule 2(c), supplied before a distance contract under regulation 13, requires the geographical address where the trader is established. Schedule 2(e) separately addresses a different business address for complaints. E-Commerce regulation 6 requires the provider's geographic establishment address to be easily accessible. Its establishment concept concerns real economic activity through a fixed establishment, rather than the location of hosting equipment [S01-S02, S54-S55].

Companies House permits an appropriate registered-office service. That permission does not establish that a mail-handling address satisfies consumer establishment duties [S03]. Keep the company's required registered-office disclosure, the actual establishment analysis and any separate complaints address distinct.

**Uncertainty and next step:** I did not locate an on-point authoritative UK decision or official worked example clearing a sole-director UK company with this exact foreign-management arrangement. That is a research gap, not proof that no such authority exists. Ask the solicitor to determine establishment and the territorial reach of the E-Commerce Regulations. There is no blanket Companies House rule requiring public disclosure of the director's home; consumer disclosure may need separate analysis if a home is the actual business establishment. A genuine business office or coworking establishment is a possible alternative to evaluate; a correspondence-only service must not be presented as an operational establishment without a basis.

**Sources/date:** [S01-S03, S54-S55]: 2013/2002 legislation and current Companies House guidance; accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on the distinct duties; Medium on the correct address for HCA.**

## 2. UK: services or digital content, and refunds?

**Short answer:** The sale being online, recurring or described as “learning” does not settle classification. Business Companion identifies apps, software and streamed/downloaded material as potential digital content. Human tuition or an individual review may instead supply a service. A package can contain both [S08]. VAT uses a separate “electronically supplied services” analysis; do not substitute that test for the CCR test [S44].

| Toolkit | Strong digital-content candidate for downloadable templates, documents or software | Whether it includes live coaching, commissioned work or a continuing service |
| Evinca review | Service candidate where a person assesses an application | Deliverables, review process, timing and what any fee purchases |

For services, regulation 36 allows a properly informed, expressly requested early start and proportionate payment for lawful supply before cancellation. It is not an automatic usage charge. For digital content, regulation 37 has consent, acknowledgment and confirmation requirements; absent required safeguards, cancellation-period content may carry no charge [S05-S07].

A voluntary 3-day full-refund promise can add rights. It cannot remove an applicable 14-day statutory right. Digital content is not generally dealt with by copying service pro-rata deductions. Faulty or misdescribed content and deficient services also have statutory remedies separate from change-of-mind cancellation [S08].

**Sources/date:** [S05-S08], 2013 legislation and Business Companion guidance updated June 2026; [S44], HMRC guidance, accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on the legal distinction; Medium on each product pending the classification brief.**

## 4. UK: DMCCA subscription regime and commencement?

**Short answer:** Do not describe commencement or the detailed regulations as confirmed by this report. The legislation retrieved still labels Part 4 Chapter 2 prospective and the Consumer Subscription Contracts (Cancellation and Information) Regulations 2026 as a draft not yet made. The draft proposes 1 January 2027. Guidance was published on 19 October 2026; the Commons order paper of 27 October still listed approval motions. The Act/draft framework includes clear pre-contract information, reminders before trial/concession conversion, periodic reminders for rolling subscriptions, an additional reminder for annual or longer renewal, and straightforward exit, online where signup is online. Timing is tied to a reasonable opportunity to avoid liability; a single generic “30-day annual email” is not an established complete solution [S10-S12].

A new 14-day renewal cooling-off right is envisaged after a concessionary period and specified annual-or-longer renewals, rather than every monthly renewal. Draft digital-content-only initial waiver rules do not simply erase renewal rights or cover all mixed contracts [S11]. Treat new-contract/transitional coverage as an implementation item. The one-off passes are not subscriptions merely because access continues; assess whether the contract creates automatic continuation/payment liability.

## 5. Saudi Arabia: e-commerce law and registrations?

**Short answer:** Saudi E-Commerce Law article 2 covers providers inside the Kingdom and certain providers outside it offering products/services inside the Kingdom. Operating the business from Saudi Arabia therefore requires analysis beyond the company's UK registration. Article 3 distinguishes the business place, practitioner address/residence and technical hosting location [S14]. The exact provider/entity and activities need local assessment; do not infer that every overseas seller needs a Saudi company merely because a Saudi customer can buy.

Maroof is an outdated verification route: the Ministry of Commerce announced replacement by the Saudi Business Center's Business Platform on 29 March 2023. Published verification requirements include a valid commercial registration or eligible professional certificate and an associated commercial bank account [S15]. The updated Investment Law uses foreign-investor registration before investment; describing every case simply as needing the old “MISA licence” is imprecise [S16].

**Local questions:** Is HCA conducting an investment/business activity in Saudi Arabia through its director? Is a branch, entity, CR or investment registration required? Does the director's residence/work status permit it? Are education, e-learning or professional-training permissions needed for these particular products? Does the store qualify for verification? None is resolved merely by UK incorporation or by obtaining a trust badge.

**Sources/date:** [S14], law issued 10 July/published 24 July 2019; [S15], ministry notice 29 March 2023; [S16], updated Investment Law, accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on the published rules; Medium on HCA's required registrations.**

## 6. Saudi Arabia: PDPL, transfers, DPO and registration?

**Short answer:** Article 2 extends to processing inside Saudi Arabia of personal data concerning individuals, and to processing outside Saudi Arabia concerning individuals residing there. Commercial processing from Saudi Arabia can therefore be relevant even if users are elsewhere. The personal/family exemption is not a general exemption for a one-director business [S17]. Verify the actual access, devices, logs, support workflows and controller/processor roles.

Transfers need a valid purpose and the prescribed protection route. Saudi transfer rules provide adequacy and, where applicable, safeguards such as the Saudi standard contractual clauses or approved binding corporate rules, subject to conditions and risk-assessment duties. A UK IDTA does not by itself establish Saudi compliance [S18].

DPO duties depend on the statutory/rules triggers, including relevant large-scale monitoring or core sensitive-data processing, rather than headcount alone [S19]. National-register rules include categories such as core personal-data activity and sensitive-data processing. A separate external-entity registration route exists; incorporation abroad is not a sufficient reason to assume no registration [S20, S53].

**Uncertainty:** Health-related submissions, Lumen prompts, identifiable application evidence, payment/account records and foreign hosting must be mapped. Confirm the registration category, DPO need, impact assessment and transfers with Saudi counsel. Do not certify “device-only” journal/mood storage without technical evidence.

**Sources/date:** [S17], PDPL 2021 as amended 2023; [S18-S20, S53], SDAIA rules/portal, accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on territorial rule; Medium on HCA's specific DPO, registration and transfer duties.**

## 7. Saudi Arabia: VAT and thresholds?

**Short answer:** For a Saudi-resident taxable business, the published mandatory threshold is SAR375,000, with voluntary registration from SAR187,500, applying the relevant preceding/expected 12-month tests. A nonresident making Saudi taxable supplies for which it must account for VAT generally has no turnover threshold. A qualifying reverse-charge B2B supply is different from a direct B2C sale [S21].

Electronic services are assessed by the relevant Saudi use/customer-location rules. The current standard VAT rate is 15%; an older digital-economy guide contains historic 5% examples, which must not be used as the current rate [S22-S23]. “Educational” branding is not sufficient evidence of an exemption.

The director's management activities may affect whether the business is resident/established for VAT, but that classification must be made under VAT rules, separately from corporate-income-tax residence. It is unsafe to choose the nonresident or resident route solely from the UK incorporation certificate. UK non-registration does not remove Saudi VAT obligations.

**Sources/date:** [S21-S22], ZATCA VAT guides; [S23], current ZATCA rate guidance, all accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on standard rate and general thresholds; Medium on supplier status, place of supply and product treatment.**

## 8. Saudi Arabia: corporate residence, PE, zakat/income tax?

**Short answer:** Saudi Income Tax Law article 3 treats a company as resident where it is formed under Saudi law or its principal management is in Saudi Arabia. A UK company can therefore raise a Saudi residence question without Saudi incorporation [S24]. ZATCA separately identifies resident capital companies' non-Saudi ownership interests and nonresidents' PE/Saudi-source income within the income-tax framework. Zakat/income-tax treatment depends on ownership and status; neither should be assumed without the shareholding facts [S25].

The UK-Saudi treaty contains a corporate place-of-effective-management tie-breaker and PE provisions covering matters such as a fixed place, place of management or office, relevant contracting agents, and a specific service-PE time test. The service time test is not a universal safe harbour for every type of PE [S26]. Having no employees does not itself eliminate these issues when the director performs the business activity.

**Next step:** Give the accountant actual work locations/dates, decision records, contracts, banking authority, ownership and premises arrangements. Determine residence before assuming the case is only about PE; assess treaty relief and any registration/filing obligations, including pre-revenue activity.

**Sources/date:** [S24], Saudi Income Tax Law; [S25], ZATCA; [S26], HMRC synthesised treaty updated 19 February 2021; accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on management as a statutory connecting factor; Medium on HCA's residence/PE and liabilities.**

## 9. Jordan: PDPL scope, transfers and registration?

**Short answer:** Jordan's Personal Data Protection Law No.24 of 2023 is operative; official material gives 17 March 2024 as the effective date and the existing-processing transition ended in March 2025. MoDEE's FAQ describes protection of citizens/residents within Jordan. It does not conclusively resolve this precise arrangement of a foreign company processing only foreign users' data through a director in Jordan [S27-S28]. Treat that territorial edge as a local-advice question, not an exemption.

Articles 14-15 regulate transfers/recipient processing, including overseas protection levels and specified exceptions. The use of a UK/EU cloud provider does not by itself prove compliance. Article 11 contains broad DPO triggers, including sensitive data, financial information and transferring databases abroad. A one-person business should not assume a small-business exemption [S27].

MoDEE now lists register instructions and related implementing materials; the official FAQ describes controller/processor registration. The applicable category, deadline and DPO entry must be verified against the current registry process [S28-S29].

**Next step:** Obtain a local view on HCA's territorial scope; map account, payment, support, AI and health-related data, foreign access/hosting, transfer mechanisms, registration and DPO requirements. Verify any claimed adequacy route rather than assuming the UK is automatically accepted.

**Sources/date:** [S27], law 2023; [S28-S29], official FAQ and implementing-materials register, accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High that the law and implementing duties are operative; Medium/Unresolved on this all-foreign-user fact pattern.**

## 10. Jordan: general sales tax on digital services?

Jordan's general sales tax standard rate is 16%, subject to the actual statutory classification/exemptions [S32].

ISTD's published ordinary service-sector registration threshold is JOD30,000 over 12 months [S33]. However, the primary material retrieved did not clearly establish whether that threshold also applies to the special nonresident electronic-services route. The existence of the portal and an invitation to register are not sufficient to prove either a zero threshold or a JOD30,000 threshold for that route.

**Unresolved:** Obtain ISTD's written answer on the special-route threshold, taxable turnover measurement, registration date and B2C/B2B treatment. If HCA is actually managed/operated from Jordan, first determine whether the nonresident/no-branch route is available; domestic GST registration, invoicing and filing may need assessment instead. Do not configure a first-sale rule or a JOD30,000 exemption on assumption.

**Sources/date:** [S30-S31], platform and official 10 January 2024 announcement; [S32], Sales Tax Law; [S33], official threshold notice 20 September 2021; accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on the scheme, general rate and ordinary threshold; Unresolved on the special nonresident threshold and HCA route.**

## 11. Jordan: corporate residence, PE and treaty?

**Short answer:** Jordan's Income Tax Law includes principal/effective management in its legal-person residence criteria. A UK company managed from Jordan therefore raises a Jordanian residence question. The law's source and business-income provisions also need separate analysis; “resident” should not be translated into an unqualified claim that every worldwide receipt is taxed identically [S34].

The UK-Jordan treaty article 4(3) uses place of effective management for a dual-resident non-individual. Article 5 addresses PE, including a fixed place and relevant contracting-agent activity; article 7 allocates business profits by reference to PE where applicable [S35]. Jordan's deposited MLI position reserves the whole of MLI article 4, so the MLI does not simply replace this treaty's corporate tie-breaker with a competent-authority-agreement test [S36]. Other matched MLI modifications still need checking.

**Next step:** Establish whether residence arises first, then assess source, PE, attribution and relief. Provide records of where the sole director actually manages the business. Merely retaining UK incorporation, banking or a mail address does not determine effective management.

**Sources/date:** [S34], Jordan Income Tax Law; [S35], treaty signed 22 July 2001, in force 24 March 2002; [S36], Jordan MLI position deposited 29 September 2020; accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on the treaty text/reservation; Medium on HCA's residence, PE and tax base.**

## 12. UK: is an incorporated company always UK tax-resident?

**Short answer:** No. UK incorporation generally creates UK corporate tax residence, but the treaty nonresident exception can apply where a relevant treaty treats the company as resident elsewhere. HMRC expressly discusses that exception [S37-S38]. Foreign management therefore needs analysis; it does not automatically end UK filing obligations or the company's legal existence.

The current HMRC synthesised UK-Saudi text retains the corporate place-of-effective-management rule. The UK-Jordan text has the same type of rule, and Jordan's MLI article 4 reservation matters [S26, S35-S36]. Do not assume that all UK treaties now use the same mutual-agreement tie-breaker.

For a sole-director business, records of where key management and commercial decisions are made and implemented are especially important. There is no general corporate-residence “183-day” test that resolves this case merely by counting the director's days. Regular work across Saudi Arabia and Jordan may raise competing claims. The two UK treaties do not by themselves resolve every Saudi-Jordan overlap.

**Next step:** Commission a coordinated residence analysis, with domestic law, both UK treaties, any relevant Saudi-Jordan treaty, periods of residence and possible migration/relief consequences. Keep the director's personal tax residence separate from company residence.

**Sources/date:** [S37-S38], HMRC manuals, current access 28 October 2026; treaty dates at questions 8 and 11. **Confidence: High on incorporation rule/exception; Medium on the resulting residence determination.**

## 13. UK: Companies House/HMRC when the director lives abroad?

**Short answer:** A director need not live in the UK, but the company needs a qualifying UK registered office. Companies House distinguishes a public service address from the actual residential address supplied privately. Keep residential address/country-of-residence information accurate, maintain the appropriate office and registered email, and check the current identity-verification and filing requirements [S03, S39-S41]. Overseas residence is not a general exemption from accounts, confirmation statements or applicable tax returns. Business websites/order forms also need the company's registered details [S04].

**Additional material VAT issue:** HMRC says essential management/central administration or a sufficiently permanent establishment with relevant resources is needed for a UK VAT establishment. A registered, serviced or virtual office alone is insufficient. A non-established taxable person making taxable UK supplies may have to register without the normal turnover threshold [S42-S43]. This company has not been conclusively classified as such here; an accountant must assess the real establishment, place of supply and any exemption before relying on “not VAT-registered.”

Ask HMRC/advisers which addresses and residence details must be updated, what filings remain, and whether director remuneration or local management creates other reporting. UK company-law residence/address rules, corporate tax residence and VAT establishment are separate tests.

**Sources/date:** [S03-S04, S39-S43], current official guidance/manuals, accessed 28 October 2026. **Confidence: High on published requirements; Medium on HCA's VAT and tax-filing position.**

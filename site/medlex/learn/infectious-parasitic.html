02 / Engage

Does “positive” mean “disease”?

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Hook • Read the result before drawing a conclusion.

“Positive screening swab. No symptoms. Colonization documented.”

Does this automatically mean an active infection? Could transmission precautions still matter?

Reveal the key distinction

Colonization means organisms are present without causing disease at that site. A positive result must be interpreted with the specimen, test, symptoms, and clinical context. Colonized people can still transmit some organisms; additional precautions may be indicated.

04 / Foundations

The agent changes the vocabulary

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Disease names do not all follow the same word-building rules.

01

Bacteria

Single-celled organisms. Example: tuberculosis is caused by bacteria in the Mycobacterium tuberculosis complex; streptococci cause strep throat.

02

Viruses

Require host cells to replicate. Examples: influenza virus, measles virus, and HIV.

03

Fungi

Include yeasts and moulds. Mycosis means fungal infection; candidiasis and tinea are examples.

04

Parasites

Organisms living on or in a host at the host’s expense. Include protozoa, helminths, and ectoparasites.

A pathogen is a disease-causing agent. The disease name, the organism name, and the transmission route are different pieces of information.

09 / Clinical language

Presence, infection, and disease

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

These words describe different clinical states.

01

Exposure

Contact with a possible source; infection does not necessarily follow.

02

Colonization

Organisms are present without causing disease at that site.

03

Infection

An agent enters and establishes itself in the host; infection may be asymptomatic.

04

Infectious disease

Illness caused by an infectious agent.

Add timing and population language

Incubation period : time from infection to symptom onset. Endemic : usual ongoing presence in a population or area. Epidemic / outbreak : cases above the expected level. Pandemic : an epidemic extending across countries or continents, affecting many people; the term describes spread, not severity.

10 / Parasitology

Three major parasite groups

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Compare the biological category before learning the disease name.

Protozoa

Single-celled parasites.

Examples: Giardia, Plasmodium.

Helminths

Parasitic worms.

Examples: nematodes, cestodes, trematodes.

Ectoparasites

Live on or within superficial body surfaces.

Examples: lice and scabies mites.

Stylized educational schematics; organism sizes are not comparable.

11 / Parasitology

Protozoa: connect agent and disease

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Different parasites use different routes.

Agent → disease Key terminology
Giardia duodenalis → giardiasis Fecal–oral transmission; ingestion of cysts
Plasmodium species → malaria Usually transmitted by an infected female Anopheles mosquito
Trophozoite / cyst Active feeding stage / resistant stage in some protozoa
Malaria is parasitic, not viral. A vector transmits an agent; a host supports part of its life cycle.

12 / Parasitology

Worms, mites, and precise names

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Click to separate organisms from diseases.

Category Example Disease / term
Nematode (roundworm) Ascaris lumbricoides Ascariasis
Cestode (tapeworm) Taenia species Taeniasis: adult intestinal tapeworm infection
Trematode (fluke) Schistosoma species Schistosomiasis
Ectoparasitic mite Sarcoptes scabiei Scabies: a skin infestation
A route that surprises learners

Schistosoma larvae released by freshwater snails can penetrate the skin during contact with infested water. Transmission is not limited to swallowing contaminated water.

Infestation is a useful clinical word

Commonly used for ectoparasites such as scabies mites and lice. Scabies usually spreads through prolonged direct skin contact; infestation can be present before symptoms develop.

13 / Investigation

Name the test—and its limits

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Match the specimen and method to the suspected agent.

Test term What it investigates Important limit
Culture Growth of organisms from a specimen Growth can represent infection, colonization, or contamination
NAAT / PCR Nucleic acid; PCR is one type of NAAT Detection does not always prove a viable agent or active disease
Antigen / antibody tests Agent components / host immune response Meaning depends on timing and the specific test
O&P Stool ova (eggs) and parasites No single test detects every parasite
Blood smear Microscopy of blood Used for blood parasites such as malaria

AST = antimicrobial susceptibility testing. A screening test and a diagnostic assessment have different purposes.

14 / Clinical language

Bacteremia is not a synonym for sepsis

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Recognize the distinction and the urgency.

Bacteremia / bacteraemia

Bacteria in the bloodstream. It can occur without sepsis.

Viremia / viraemia

Virus in the bloodstream.

May arise from bacterial, viral, fungal, or parasitic infections. Blood cultures can be negative.

Sepsis is a medical emergency. Suspected infection with new confusion, breathing difficulty, low urine output, or circulatory deterioration requires urgent assessment.

15 / Treatment language

Match the drug class to the target

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Antimicrobial is the umbrella term.

01

Antibacterial / antibiotic

Treats selected bacterial infections; does not treat viral infections.

02

Antiviral

Acts against specific viruses; no single agent treats every virus.

03

Antifungal

Treats selected fungal infections.

04

Antiparasitic

Includes antiprotozoal and anthelmintic agents; therapy depends on the parasite.

AMR = antimicrobial resistance: organisms become able to withstand medicines intended to act against them. The organism becomes resistant, not the patient.

Susceptible / resistant are laboratory interpretation categories. Antimicrobial stewardship supports appropriate selection, use, and duration.

16 / Apply

One case. Four precise explanations.

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Fictional case • Translate without overdiagnosing.

“Diarrhea after untreated stream-water exposure. Stool Giardia antigen detected. Giardiasis diagnosed.”
1 · Identify the agent and category

Giardia is a protozoan parasite; giardiasis is the disease name.

2 · Explain the likely route

Ingestion through the fecal–oral route is plausible. The result and history do not prove the exact source of exposure.

3 · Translate the test

A stool test detected a component of Giardia. The clinician interpreted it together with the symptoms and exposure history.

4 · Explain to the patient

“Your clinician diagnosed an intestinal parasite infection. A stool test found evidence of Giardia. The treatment depends on the organism and your clinical circumstances.”

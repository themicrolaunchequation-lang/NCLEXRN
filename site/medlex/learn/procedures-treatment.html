01 / EXPLORE & APPLY

A small ending changes the action

Hook • commit to an interpretation

Trache otomy

Trache ostomy

Which ending indicates an incision—and which indicates a created opening?

Reveal the core distinction

-otomy means cutting into or making an incision. -ostomy means creating an opening. Tracheotomy describes an incision into the trachea; tracheostomy commonly describes creation of an airway opening, and the resulting opening itself. Clinical usage sometimes overlaps.

Word parts guide meaning. The operative record supplies the exact procedure and clinical context.

03 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Procedure, treatment and purpose

Choose a purpose to inspect a clinical example

Procedure

A clinical action performed for diagnosis, treatment, prevention or monitoring.

Treatment / therapy

An intervention intended to manage a condition, relieve symptoms or improve function.

One procedure may have more than one purpose. Endoscopy can inspect tissue, obtain a biopsy or deliver treatment.

05 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Removal, incision and opening

Compare the actions before memorising the examples

Excision / resection

Removal of tissue. The exact extent is specified in the procedure name or record.

A stoma

A surgically created opening. Some stomas are temporary; others are permanent.

An -ectomy does not always mean removal of the whole organ: partial and total procedures exist.

08 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Diagnostic procedure vocabulary

A sample, a view and a record provide different evidence

Biopsy

Removal of cells or tissue for examination. A biopsy does not itself mean that cancer is present.

Incisional / excisional biopsy

Incisional: sample part of a lesion. Excisional: remove the entire suspicious lump or area for examination.

Endoscopy

Inspect the inside of the body with a scope; tissue sampling or treatment may also be performed.

Electrocardiogram • ECG / EKG

A record of heart electrical activity. It is not an image of heart anatomy.

09 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Approach: how is the site reached?

The approach and the action are separate descriptors

Approach Meaning Example
Open Direct access through an incision Open surgical resection
Laparoscopic Access to the abdomen/pelvis using a scope through small incisions Laparoscopic cholecystectomy
Endoscopic Access with an endoscope through a passage or opening Endoscopic polypectomy
Percutaneous Through the skin Percutaneous needle biopsy

“Minimally invasive” describes an approach. It does not mean that the procedure has no risks.

A full name may combine approach, location and action.

10 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Analgesia, sedation and anaesthesia

Related terms with different meanings

Analgesia

Pain relief. It does not necessarily involve loss of awareness.

Sedation

Reduced alertness or awareness along a continuum. The depth and monitoring needs vary.

Anaesthesia / anesthesia

Loss of sensation. General anaesthesia also produces unconsciousness; local and regional techniques numb defined areas.

Does “local anaesthetic” mean the person must be unconscious?

No. Local anaesthesia numbs a small area. A patient may remain awake; sedation may be used separately when appropriate.

Anaesthetic = agent or adjective; anaesthesia = state. American spelling: anesthetic / anesthesia.

11 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Routes of medicine administration

Name where or how the medicine enters

Route Meaning
Oral · sublingual · buccal By mouth · under the tongue · against the inner cheek
Intravenous · IV Into a vein
Intramuscular · IM Into a muscle
Subcutaneous Into tissue beneath the skin
Inhaled Delivered into the respiratory tract by breathing it in
Topical · transdermal Applied to a surface · through skin for systemic delivery

A route is part of the medicine instruction. Topical and transdermal describe different delivery concepts.

Use the full route name when an abbreviation could be misunderstood.

12 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Indication, reaction and clear instructions

Use words that make the clinical meaning explicit

Indication / contraindication

Indication: reason to use an intervention. Contraindication: a circumstance that makes its use inappropriate or potentially harmful; some are absolute and others relative.

Adverse effect / allergy

An adverse effect is an unwanted harmful effect. An allergic reaction involves the immune system; many unwanted effects are not allergies.

Write clearly Reason
0.5 mg Include a leading zero before a decimal
5 mg Omit an unnecessary trailing zero
“units” and “daily” Avoid ambiguous U and QD abbreviations
“As needed for [specified reason]” PRN means as needed; the indication must be clear

Numeric examples illustrate notation only. They are not medicine recommendations.

13 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Treatment families and goals

Name the method, then clarify its purpose

Pharmacotherapy

Treatment with medicines. An antimicrobial acts against microorganisms; an antibiotic treats susceptible bacteria, not viral infections.

Cancer treatment terms

Radiotherapy uses radiation. Chemotherapy, targeted therapy and immunotherapy describe different treatment approaches.

Rehabilitation

Supports functioning and participation through interventions such as physiotherapy and occupational therapy.

Palliative care

Addresses suffering and quality of life in serious illness. It can accompany disease-directed treatment and is not limited to the final days of life.

14 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Before, during and after a procedure

Timing words connect the clinical story

Preoperative Before surgery

Intraoperative During surgery

Postoperative After surgery

Select a phase to explore its terminology.

Informed consent

A process involving understandable information and a voluntary decision by a person with the relevant decision-making capacity.

Aseptic / sterile

Aseptic technique aims to prevent microbial contamination. Sterile describes freedom from viable microorganisms.

Perioperative spans the period around surgery. Consent is a communication process; a signed form alone does not demonstrate understanding.

15 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Wound and supportive procedure terms

Translate the action without giving a technique

Term Plain-language meaning
Incision and drainage · I&D Open a collection and allow fluid, such as pus, to drain
Debridement Remove dead, damaged or contaminated tissue as clinically indicated
Suturing / wound closure Approximate tissue edges using stitches or another closure method
Dressing Material applied to protect or manage a wound
Haemostasis / hemostasis Control or stopping of bleeding
Irrigation Flushing or washing a body site or wound

The procedure name identifies the action; wound assessment and the treatment plan determine whether and how it is performed.

16 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Clinical language studio

Read → interpret → explain in plain language

“Laparoscopic cholecystectomy under general anaesthesia.”

Translate the record

Removal of the gallbladder using a laparoscopic approach, with general anaesthesia producing unconsciousness and loss of sensation. The approach, action and anaesthesia are distinct parts of the description.

A patient receives an endoscopic biopsy, analgesia, and rehabilitation.

Classify each item

Endoscopic biopsy: a scope-assisted diagnostic sampling procedure. Analgesia: pain relief. Rehabilitation: support for functioning and participation. The same care plan may combine several purposes.

Your exit task

Exit task: decode nephrectomy, then compare it with nephrostomy

Nephr- identifies the kidney; -ectomy indicates surgical removal. Nephrostomy creates an opening into the kidney collecting system, usually for drainage. It does not remove kidney tissue.

Explain why an endoscopy can be diagnostic and therapeutic. Then translate “subcutaneous” and rewrite an ambiguous medicine instruction in plain language.

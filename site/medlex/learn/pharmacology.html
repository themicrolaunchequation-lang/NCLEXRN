03 / PHARMACOLOGY MAP

One field. Several questions.

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Pharmacology

The science of drugs and their interactions with living systems.

Pharmacokinetics · PK

How drug concentrations change as the body absorbs, distributes and eliminates a drug.

Pharmacodynamics · PD

How a drug produces biological effects, including concentration–response relationships.

Pharmacogenomics

How genetic variation influences drug response, including some PK and PD differences.

PK asks “how much reaches where, and when?” PD asks “what response occurs?”

05 / MEDICINE LANGUAGE

Name, class, strength or dose?

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Generic / brand name

The non-proprietary name identifies the drug; a brand name identifies a marketed product.

Drug class

A grouping by action, use or structure. “Analgesic” describes a pain-relieving therapeutic role.

Strength ≠ dose

Strength: amount per dosage unit. Dose: amount administered at one time. Two fictional 100 mg tablets supply a 200 mg dose.

Dosage form / formulation

Tablet, solution or injection; immediate-release and modified-release products can deliver drug differently.

Regimen = dose, route, interval and duration. A tablet’s colour or size does not identify its strength.

06 / ADMINISTRATION TERMINOLOGY

Route changes the journey

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Oral

Swallowed; absorption commonly occurs in the gastrointestinal tract.

Intravenous · IV

Directly into a vein. No absorption step into systemic circulation; absolute bioavailability is 1.

Intramuscular · IM / subcutaneous · SC

Into muscle / beneath the skin. Drug still needs to enter the circulation from the injection site.

Topical / transdermal

Topical: applied to a body surface, often for local action. Transdermal: delivered across skin for systemic action.

First-pass metabolism occurs before systemic entry. Drug absorbed directly through sublingual mucosa avoids that initial portal passage.

09 / DISTRIBUTION & BINDING

Where does the drug go?

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Distribution

Reversible movement between blood and tissues.

Unbound / protein-bound drug

Drug can bind plasma proteins. Unbound concentration is often most relevant to membrane passage and target interaction.

Volume of distribution · Vd

An apparent volume: amount of drug in the body divided by plasma concentration. It is not a literal body compartment.

Tissue barriers

Permeability, blood flow and binding affect access. Entry into the brain is influenced by the blood–brain barrier.

Vd = amount in body ÷ plasma concentration

e.g. amount / (amount per litre) → litres

10 / METABOLISM, EXCRETION & INTERACTIONS

Change the molecule. Remove it from the body.

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Metabolism / biotransformation

Chemical change of a drug, often in the liver. Metabolites may be inactive, active or toxic.

Prodrug

A precursor converted in the body to an active drug or active metabolite.

Excretion

Removal of drug or metabolites, commonly through urine or bile. Renal means kidney-related; hepatic means liver-related.

Enzyme inhibition / induction

Reduced activity / increased enzyme expression or activity. Effects depend on the pathway and whether the active agent is parent drug or metabolite.

PK interaction: changes drug exposure. PD interaction: changes the combined response without requiring an exposure change.

12 / HALF-LIFE EXPLORER

Clearance sets the pace

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Teaching model: linear, first-order, one-compartment elimination after an instantaneous input. t½ = ln(2) × Vd / CL. This does not calculate an individual dose.

Clearance · volume / time

Effective plasma volume cleared of drug per unit time, such as L/h. It is not an amount-per-hour elimination rate.

Half-life · time

Time for concentration to fall by half during a specified elimination phase.

Simplified one-compartment, linear, first-order model; no further drug input. Increasing CL shortens half-life if Vd stays constant.

13 / ACCUMULATION & STEADY STATE

Repeated input. A changing balance.

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Original model of a constant infusion; zero starting concentration · Click to enlarge.

Steady state

Average input and elimination are balanced. Repeated intermittent doses can still produce peaks and troughs.

Loading / maintenance

Loading aims to reach a target amount sooner. Maintenance replaces ongoing elimination.

In linear first-order PK, about 4–5 half-lives gives 94–97% of the eventual level after starting a constant input. This is an approximation, not complete equilibrium.

14 / RECEPTOR INTERACTION LAB

Binding is not the same as activation.

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Affinity

The tendency of a ligand to bind to a target.

Efficacy

The capacity to generate a response in the specified system.

Agonists activate receptors. A neutral antagonist blocks agonist action without activating the receptor itself.

16 / BENEFIT, HARM & MONITORING

Use safety terms precisely.

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Adverse event / ADR

An adverse event occurs during treatment without necessarily being caused by it. An adverse drug reaction has a suspected causal relationship.

Serious / severe

Serious concerns outcomes or regulatory criteria; severe describes intensity. They are not interchangeable.

Therapeutic window / index

Window: a range supporting benefit with acceptable toxicity. Index: a ratio comparing toxic and effective dose levels, often TD50 / ED50.

Contraindication / TDM

Contraindication: a reason to avoid a treatment because of risk. Therapeutic drug monitoring uses timed drug levels with clinical interpretation.

Not every unwanted effect is an allergy. TDM results depend on sampling time, the regimen, the assay and the clinical situation.

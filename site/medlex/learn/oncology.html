03 / Decode

Read the parts. Confirm the whole word.

Violet = prefix · teal = root / combining form · coral = suffix

Word part Meaning Example
onc/o · carcin/o Tumor · cancer oncology · carcinoma
neo- + -plasia New + formation / growth neoplasia → new, abnormal growth
aden/o Gland adenocarcinoma → glandular epithelial cancer
-oma · -logy · -ectomy Tumor · study of · surgical removal lipoma · oncology · lumpectomy

onc/o + -logy → study of tumors; the clinical field of cancer care.

“-oma” does not establish benignity: lipoma is benign; melanoma and lymphoma are malignant. Learn accepted meanings, not just literal parts.

06 / Investigate

Three questions behind the report

Sample → examine → characterise

01

Biopsy

Removal of cells or tissue for diagnostic examination. A sample is not automatically a cancer diagnosis.

02

Histopathology

Microscopic examination of tissue: helps identify tumor type and other pathological features.

03

Biomarker testing

Looks for genes, proteins or other features that may guide treatment selection in appropriate cancers.

Somatic ≠ inherited

A tumor’s acquired molecular change is different from a germline variant that can be inherited. Tumor testing may suggest a need for separate hereditary testing.

A positive biomarker does not guarantee that a treatment will work. Eligibility and interpretation depend on the cancer and clinical context.

07 / Describe

Grade describes biology. Stage describes extent.

Return to the opening question

How abnormal does it look?

Often reflects differentiation and other cancer-specific features, such as mitotic activity. High grade commonly suggests more aggressive behavior.

Grading systems differ; a grade number is not universal across cancers.

Where is it, and how far has it spread?

Click T, N or M to decode the staging components.

TNM is used for many solid cancers. Blood and some other cancers use different systems.

A high-grade cancer may be early stage. Never convert a TNM code into a stage group without the cancer type and the applicable staging system / edition.

08 / Treat

Treatment names describe the approach.

Learn the modality, then the timing

Term Core meaning
Surgery / resection Physical removal of tumor or tissue.
Radiotherapy Radiation used to damage cancer cells.
Chemotherapy Drug treatment that kills cancer cells or limits growth, often by affecting cell division.
Targeted therapy Acts on specific molecular targets involved in cancer growth.
Immunotherapy Uses or modifies immune responses against cancer.
Hormone / endocrine therapy Blocks or reduces hormones that support some cancers.

Before the main treatment, often surgery.

After the main treatment to reduce recurrence risk.

Decode remission, recurrence and prognosis

Remission: reduction or disappearance of cancer signs and symptoms. Complete remission means all have disappeared; cancer may still remain in the body. Recurrence: cancer returns after a period when it was undetectable. Prognosis: the likely course or outcome, not a certainty for one person.

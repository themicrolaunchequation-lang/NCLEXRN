Same person. Different-looking records.

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

Record A

“Anaemia. FBC requested. Takes paracetamol.”

Record B

“Anemia. CBC requested. Takes acetaminophen.”
What changed—and what still needs checking?

Anaemia/anemia are spelling variants. FBC/CBC name the routine blood count; confirm the exact ordered panel. Paracetamol/acetaminophen name the same active ingredient, but the product, strength and formulation still need verification.

A word may change while the concept stays the same. A familiar word may also conceal a real difference.

06 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Titles are clues—not automatic equivalents

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

UK: consultant

A senior doctor with responsibility for patient care in the relevant specialty.

US: attending physician

A physician responsible for care who may supervise resident physicians.

UK surgeons: Mr / Ms / Miss / Mrs

Some surgeons use these titles by tradition despite being medically qualified.

“Registrar” and “resident physician” can describe training roles in different systems. They do not establish a one-to-one grade match.

For a handover, state the person’s specialty and responsibility. For credentials, check the relevant regulator and employer.

Common wording in UK practice.

CBC

Complete blood count

Common wording in US practice.

Same broad test concept

Confirm analytes, units, specimen, reference intervals and laboratory method where relevant.

09 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Generic names travel better than brands

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

INN

WHO International Nonproprietary Names identify active pharmaceutical substances; nationally approved names may still differ.

Brand / trade name

A product name chosen by a manufacturer. Similar or identical brands in different countries may contain different ingredients.

Identify the actual product

Confirm active ingredient(s), strength, dosage form, route, release characteristics and local product information.

A brand name looks familiar. Is that enough?

No. Check the product’s active ingredients and formulation against authoritative product information. Do not infer interchangeability from the name alone.

11 / EXPLORE & APPLY

A number without a unit can mislead

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

Glucose may be reported in mmol/L or mg/dL. Read the actual unit rather than inferring it from the country.

For glucose: mmol/L × 18.02 ≈ mg/dL.

Preserve the source value

Keep the original number and unit alongside a converted value.

Analyte-specific conversion

Do not use the glucose factor for cholesterol, creatinine or other analytes.

Interpretation is separate

A conversion does not establish a diagnosis or replace the source lab’s reference information.

Abbreviations are especially local

Test names

FBC and CBC often refer to the same broad blood-count concept; verify the requested components.

Ambiguous short forms

“MS” has multiple meanings. In medication communication, use the intended full drug name.

Local policy

Follow the receiving organization’s approved terminology. Avoid error-prone shortened forms identified by medication-safety guidance.

Use the verified active-ingredient name, full product details and explicit instructions from the source record. Expand unfamiliar abbreviations rather than guessing.

Shared concepts help—codes still need checking

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

SNOMED CT

Clinical concepts can have different preferred terms or acceptable synonyms in different language or dialect contexts.

ICD-10-CM

The US clinical modification is used to code medical diagnoses. A country-specific code set is not just a spelling variant.

Exchange precisely

State the concept, code system, edition/version and relevant date when sharing coded information.

Do not substitute a code from a different national modification or version merely because the diagnosis wording looks similar.

16 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Rewrite the handover without losing information

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

“Anaemia; FBC requested. Paracetamol on medication list. Glucose 5.0. Reviewed 03/04/2026.”
1 · What can be restated?

Anaemia can be written anemia for a US-style record. FBC can be described as full/complete blood count, with the exact components retained. Paracetamol is also called acetaminophen; the product details still need verification.

2 · What cannot be safely inferred?

The glucose unit and the date interpretation are missing or ambiguous. Do not assume either from the spelling or apparent country of origin.

3 · After the sender confirms the missing details

If the source confirms mmol/L and 3 April: “Anemia; full blood count requested [components as ordered]. Paracetamol (acetaminophen) [verified product details]. Glucose 5.0 mmol/L (approximately 90.1 mg/dL). Reviewed 3 April 2026.”

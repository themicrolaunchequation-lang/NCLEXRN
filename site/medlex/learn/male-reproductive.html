07 / Colour-coded word parts

The endings carry the action

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

oligo- PREFIX · FEW sperm ROOT · SPERM -ia SUFFIX · CONDITION

-itis / -algia

Inflammation / pain: orchitis, orchialgia.

-ectomy / -pexy

Removal / fixation: orchiectomy, orchiopexy.

-genesis / -cele

Formation / swelling or hernia: spermatogenesis, hydrocele.

a-, an- / dys-

Without / difficult, painful or abnormal: azoospermia, dysuria.

Coral = prefix · blue = root / combining form · violet = suffix. Read the suffix first, then confirm the clinical definition. Not every term divides neatly into predictable pieces.

09 / Connect words with function

Sperm, semen & hormones

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Spermatogenesis

Production of sperm cells in the seminiferous tubules of the testes.

Semen

Ejaculated fluid normally containing sperm and secretions from accessory glands.

LH & testosterone

Luteinising hormone stimulates Leydig cells to produce testosterone.

FSH & Sertoli cells

Follicle-stimulating hormone acts on Sertoli cells. FSH and intratesticular testosterone support sperm production.

Sperm = cells. Semen = the fluid mixture. Erection = penile engorgement. Ejaculation = emission and expulsion of semen. These are distinct concepts.

11 / Localise the term

Inflammation: where is it?

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Prostatitis

Inflammation of the prostate. Prostatitis syndromes include bacterial and nonbacterial categories.

Epididymitis

Inflammation of the epididymis. Orchitis involves the testis.

Epididymo-orchitis

Inflammation involving both the epididymis and testis.

Balanitis / urethritis

Inflammation of the glans penis / urethra, respectively.

Inflammation does not automatically identify the organism or mean a sexually transmitted infection. Sudden severe testicular pain needs immediate emergency assessment because testicular torsion is possible.

12 / Compare the anatomy

Structural changes have distinct names

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Cryptorchidism

One or both testes have not descended into the scrotum.

Hydrocele

Fluid accumulation around a testis, usually within the tunica vaginalis.

Varicocele

Abnormally dilated veins in the pampiniform plexus of the spermatic cord.

Testicular torsion

Twisting of the spermatic cord that can impair testicular blood flow.

Hydrocele concerns fluid. Varicocele concerns veins. Cryptorchidism concerns position. Torsion concerns twisting and blood supply.

13 / Use precise descriptions

Function & fertility are different

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Erectile dysfunction · ED

Difficulty achieving or maintaining an erection sufficient for sexual activity.

Infertility

Difficulty achieving pregnancy. Male-factor evaluation considers more than erectile function.

Azoospermia

No sperm detected in the ejaculate after appropriate laboratory assessment.

Oligozoospermia

Low sperm concentration. Asthenozoospermia concerns motility; teratozoospermia concerns morphology.

An individual can have erections and still have impaired fertility. A single semen result does not fully predict fertility or identify the cause.

14 / Compare clinical meanings

The prostate: growth is not a diagnosis

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Conceptual diagram. No size or flow measurements implied.

Benign prostatic hyperplasia · BPH

Noncancerous increase in prostate cell number. It may contribute to urinary symptoms.

Hyperplasia versus hypertrophy

Hyperplasia = increased cell number. Hypertrophy = increased cell size.

Prostate cancer

A malignancy. BPH and cancer are distinct conditions and may coexist.

The prostate surrounds the proximal urethra. Enlargement can narrow this passage. Urinary symptoms alone do not identify the cause.

15 / Tests and abbreviations

Investigations: what is assessed?

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Semen analysis

Assesses features such as sperm concentration, motility and morphology, alongside semen volume.

PSA blood test

Measures prostate-specific antigen. BPH, inflammation and cancer can all raise PSA.

DRE

Digital rectal examination. Digital refers to a finger used to assess the prostate through the rectum.

Ultrasound & biopsy

Ultrasound produces images. A biopsy obtains tissue for pathological examination.

A raised PSA is not a cancer diagnosis. Urologists interpret symptoms, examination and tests together. Screening decisions require current guidance and individual discussion.

16 / Surgical language

Procedures: removal, fixation or repair?

Explore the detail panels. Recall the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Orchiectomy / orchiopexy

Removal of a testis / surgical fixation of a testis.

Vasectomy / vasovasostomy

Occlusion or division of the vasa deferentia for contraception / reconnection of divided vas segments.

Prostatectomy / TURP

Removal of part or all of the prostate / transurethral resection of prostate tissue.

Circumcision

Surgical removal of the prepuce, also called the foreskin.

Vasectomy is intended as permanent contraception. It does not remove the testes, does not protect against STIs and is not immediately effective. Follow-up semen testing confirms clearance according to the clinical protocol.

19 / Consolidation & exit task

A compact clinical vocabulary

Find the structure

orch/o: testis · epididym/o: epididymis · prostat/o: prostate.

Read the ending

-itis: inflammation · -ectomy: removal · -pexy: fixation.

Check the distinction

Sperm and semen differ. ED and infertility differ. BPH and prostate cancer differ.

Translate precisely

A symptom is not a cause. A test result is not a diagnosis. Procedure names do not explain every indication.

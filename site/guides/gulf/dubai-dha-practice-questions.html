10 free DHA practice questions

Question 1. A DHA-licensed nurse is allocated a nonurgent procedure that the nurse has never been trained to perform. What is the best response?

Answer: B. The request does not establish competence. Arranging qualified help protects the patient without abandoning care. Trap to avoid: Remote instructions do not establish assessed competence.

Question 2. A nurse supervising care in a DHA-licensed facility plans to delegate a routine clinical task. Which check is essential?

Answer: B. Willingness and availability do not establish authority or ability. Delegation must fit the person and the task. Trap to avoid: Workload matters only after role and ability are established.

Question 3. In Dubai, a nurse delegates an authorized task to a competent team member. Which follow-up is most appropriate?

Answer: B. Delegation does not end the need for oversight. Outcome review helps identify care that needs adjustment. Trap to avoid: Waiting for a complaint misses earlier signs of incomplete care.

Question 4. In a DHA-licensed unit, a patient awaiting an elective procedure cannot explain what the procedure involves despite a signed form. What should the nurse do first?

Answer: C. A signature alone does not show informed agreement. The misunderstanding must be addressed before the elective procedure proceeds. Trap to avoid: A form does not resolve the stated misunderstanding.

Question 5. A capable adult in Dubai asks to discuss an alternative treatment. A family member prefers the proposed plan. What should the nurse do?

Answer: A. Care should support the patient’s informed participation. Family involvement should not prevent the patient from receiving information. Trap to avoid: Family preference does not displace a capable adult’s voice.

Question 6. In a Dubai clinic, a caller requests an adult patient’s test results and claims to be a relative. Permission to disclose has not been established. What is best?

Answer: C. The request alone does not authorize disclosure. Identity and a valid basis for access must both be established. Trap to avoid: Selected results are still confidential information.

Question 7. In a Dubai ward, a stable patient’s sensitive history is being discussed within hearing of unrelated visitors. What is the best action?

Answer: A. Avoidable exposure of personal information should be reduced. Omitting a name does not make an identifiable bedside conversation private. Trap to avoid: Room context can still identify the patient.

Question 8. A stable patient in a DHA-licensed unit requests a same-gender practitioner for an intimate examination. What is the best initial response?

Answer: C. Preferences should be discussed individually and respected where feasible. The nurse must also account for the urgency of care. Trap to avoid: An individual preference must not be generalized without discussion.

Question 9. After administering prescribed analgesia in Dubai, which record best supports safe continuing care?

Answer: D. The record should show what was done and how the patient responded. Intended outcomes must not be recorded as observed facts. Trap to avoid: A broad completion statement omits important clinical details.

Question 10. A patient in Dubai needs more oxygen than earlier in the shift. Which handover is most appropriate?

Answer: B. A new oxygen requirement is a relevant change in condition. The receiving team needs the current situation and outstanding actions. Trap to avoid: The chart does not replace communication of a significant change.

DHA exam questions people ask

How many questions are in the Dubai DHA nursing exam?

150 multiple-choice questions in 3 hours. The 3 hours includes registration and a short introduction, so your answering time is a little less.

What is the DHA exam pass mark?

The published pass mark is 50%.

How many times can I take the DHA exam?

General DHA licensing rules apply: three attempts across UAE authorities, a fourth only with permission, and a two-year block after the third failure before new attempts. Sources differ on details; confirm with DHA.

Carecraft · Accessible information

Health literacy is a shared responsibility

Reduce jargon, information overload and the hidden effort of navigating care.

What you will practise

  • Design for everyone
  • Make the next action visible
  • Check your explanation

Key point: An explanation succeeds when the person can use it.

Try a real situation

During a discharge talk at 16:30, a woman who has followed everything so far goes quiet when you say “PRN”, “U&Es” and “OPD follow-up”. She has her coat on and her lift is waiting outside. What helps most?

A. Swap the abbreviations for everyday words, focus on one next action, and ask her to say it back.

B. Slow down and say the abbreviations more clearly, so she can pick up the terms as you go along.

C. Carry on at the same pace, since she was following well and has not asked you to stop or explain.

The answer, with why each option is right or wrong, is in Carecraft.

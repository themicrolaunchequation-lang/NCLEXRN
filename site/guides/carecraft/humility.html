Carecraft · Intellectual humility

Be willing to update your view

Hold a working explanation while staying open to information that challenges it.

What you will practise

  • Name what you do not know
  • Invite a different view
  • Update and communicate

Key point: Changing your mind can be a sign of careful thinking.

Try a real situation

You are the registrar presenting a patient on the ward round. Your first thought was a urine infection, but the blood results do not quite fit and the consultant is waiting for your plan. Which statement shows useful humility?

A. “My working view is a urine infection, but the bloods don’t fit, so let’s check what else it could be.”

B. “I’ve seen this many times and I’m confident, so let’s only revisit it if she gets any worse.”

C. “It could be a urine infection, though honestly I’m not really sure about anything on this case.”

The answer, with why each option is right or wrong, is in Carecraft.

Carecraft · Patient safety

Small checks. Safer care.

Build safer habits around medications, transitions, and everyday clinical decisions.

What to do

  1. Pause before acting. Use the identifiers required by your organization. Check allergy information and the intended task against the appropriate record; location or familiarity is not a substitute for identification.
  2. Reconcile at transitions. Compare the best available medication history with the current plan. Clarify unexplained differences with the responsible prescriber or pharmacist; do not guess. Involve the patient or caregiver.
  3. Make uncertainty visible. If an order, dose, route, or product is unclear, pause and clarify through your local process before proceeding. Report near misses through the organizational system so the team can learn.

Key point: A pause to resolve uncertainty is a safety action, not a delay to apologize for.

Try a real situation

A man arrives on the ward from a care home. The transfer sheet lists a blood-thinning tablet that his daughter says was stopped last week, and the GP summary does not mention it. The drug round starts in ten minutes. What best addresses the uncertainty?

Reconciliation resolves a conflict between records and the patient’s account; it does not pick a winner by guesswork. Pausing the one uncertain item protects him while the answer is found.

Carecraft · Recognition & escalation

Recognise a change. Escalate clearly.

Make changes, urgency and responsibility explicit without waiting for certainty.

What you will practise

  • Notice the change
  • Use the immediate local route
  • Keep the loop open until action is clear

Key point: Communicate significant concern before certainty arrives.

Try a real situation

During a routine 20:00 handover on an older-people ward, you notice Mrs Pike’s breathing is faster and she seems newly confused. The coordinator is halfway through the SBAR for bed 12 and the oncoming team is waiting. What takes priority?

A. Interrupt and use the immediate local escalation pathway for Mrs Pike, then return to the handover.

B. Let the coordinator finish a clear SBAR for bed 12, so the handover is complete before you speak up.

C. Write the change on the handover sheet and make sure it is raised at the next routine review.

The answer, with why each option is right or wrong, is in Carecraft.

Evidence and guidance

  • Starmer et al. · NEJM · 2014

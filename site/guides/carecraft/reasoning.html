Carecraft · Clinical reasoning

Think clearly under uncertainty

Recognize assumptions, seek disconfirming evidence, and ask for a second view.

What to do

  1. Separate observations from interpretation. State what you know, how reliable it is, and what is missing. Do not let an early label replace ongoing assessment.
  2. Take a diagnostic pause. Ask: “What else could explain this? What does not fit? What important alternative should I not miss?” Use current, locally approved pathways and decision support within your scope.
  3. Reassess and safety-net. Discuss uncertainty and the follow-up plan with the responsible team and patient. Specify what change warrants review and who will act on pending results. Seek senior help when concern exceeds your role or confidence.

Key point: Confidence is useful; a clear plan for uncertainty is safer.

Try a real situation

A man in the emergency department was labelled “anxiety, chest tightness” at triage. An hour later his observations have changed and he says the pain now spreads to his arm. What best supports a safe reassessment?

Naming the mismatch out loud and opening up alternatives counters anchoring on the first label. It keeps the decision with the responsible team.

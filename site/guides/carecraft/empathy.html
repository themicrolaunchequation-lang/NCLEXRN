Carecraft · Person-centered care

Empathy in everyday practice

Bring curiosity, respect, and compassion to the moments that need them most.

What to do

  1. Notice without assuming. Observe words, tone, and hesitation. Ask “What concerns you most?” rather than inferring feelings from appearance, age, culture, or diagnosis.
  2. Acknowledge the experience. Try “That sounds like a lot to manage.” Allow silence. Validation does not require agreeing with every request or making promises outside your role.
  3. Invite partnership. Explore the person’s priorities, practical barriers, and preferred involvement in decisions. Share options honestly, including uncertainty, and agree on an achievable next step.

Key point: Empathy is curiosity put into words and followed by action.

Try a real situation

Mrs Hughes, 78, was given a new daily exercise plan after her fall. At review she sighs and says, “It’s all a bit much, really.” You have ten minutes. What is the most useful response before you offer any solution?

Naming the feeling and asking which part is hard shows you are curious rather than assuming. Her answer then tells you where to adapt the plan.

Reflect

Think of a time you made an assumption. What curious question could you ask instead? Do not include identifying details.

Evidence and guidance

  • Howick et al. · Journal of the Royal Society of Medicine · 2018

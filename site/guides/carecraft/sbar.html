Carecraft · Team communication

A handover that gets heard

Use SBAR and closed-loop communication to make the next step unmistakable.

What to do

  1. Lead with situation and urgency. Identify yourself, your role, the patient using approved identifiers, and the immediate concern. State urgency clearly. Escalate emergencies immediately through local procedures.
  2. Give relevant background and assessment. Keep background concise. Share recent changes, relevant context, and your assessment within your scope. Separate observed facts from interpretation.
  3. Make a clear recommendation. Ask explicitly for the review or action you need and by when. Confirm the recipient’s understanding, ownership, and escalation plan if help does not arrive.

Key point: A good handover transfers responsibility, not just information.

Try a real situation

At 02:00 you give an SBAR about a patient whose breathing has worsened. The on-call doctor says, “OK, noted, thanks,” and starts to hang up without saying whether she is coming. What best establishes a shared plan?

Ownership, timing and a contingency turn an acknowledged message into a shared plan. Asking before she hangs up is the last chance to close the loop.

ROOT + VOWEL

A root is not
a combining form.

The vowel helps join components; it adds no separate clinical meaning.

Select a labelled part to reveal its job.
THE WHOLE TERM

gastr/o/scopy → gastroscopy. Compare gastr/itis, where the vowel is omitted before -itis.

JOINING RULES

Use the vowel
where it belongs.

General rules guide the spelling; the accepted word remains the authority.

01

Before a consonant

gastr/o/scopy → usually keep the vowel.

02

Before a vowel

gastr/itis → usually omit the vowel.

03

Between roots

gastr/o/enter/itis → retain the linking vowel, even before a vowel-starting root.

PREFIX + ROOT

sub/hepat/ic has no inserted vowel after sub-. Check exceptions in a reliable reference.

Start with the ending.
Finish with meaning.

Follow the same four steps each time.

01

1 / Suffix

02

2 / Roots

03

3 / Prefix

04

4 / Verify

REGIONAL STYLE

Haematology/hematology and anaemia/anemia are accepted variants. Use a consistent style; do not assume every word with the same ending forms its plural in the same way.

EPONYMS & ABBREVIATIONS

Not every term
is a word-part puzzle.

Some terms need a different route to understanding.

01

Eponyms

Apgar score is named for a person; body-system roots do not decode it.

02

Initialisms

ECG expands to electrocardiogram. Expand unfamiliar shortened forms.

03

Context

MS can have different meanings. Never guess an ambiguous medication abbreviation.

A SAFER HABIT

Use the full intended term when ambiguity could matter, and follow approved local abbreviation policy.

CLINICAL PRECISION

Literal meaning
has limits.

Decoding a word is a starting point.

01

A finding is not its cause

Hepatomegaly identifies liver enlargement; it does not explain why it occurred.

02

A suffix is not a prognosis

-oma alone does not establish whether a mass is benign or malignant.

03

A word is not a complete diagnosis

Clinical meaning depends on history, examination, investigations and the full context.

THE HABIT

Decode → verify → explain. Avoid an inference the term does not support.

SOURCESNLM / Medical word partsOpen RN / Common suffixeseCampusOntario / Word parts in medical terms

GUIDED CASE

Gastritis.
Gastroscopy.

“The patient has gastritis and is scheduled for gastroscopy.”

1 / Separate the parts+

gastr/itis and gastr/o/scopy. Both concern the stomach.

2 / Explain the vowel+

-itis starts with a vowel, so o is usually omitted. -scopy starts with a consonant, so o remains.

3 / Explain the plan+

“Your stomach lining is inflamed. The planned procedure lets the clinician look inside using an endoscope.”

TERMINOLOGY EXERCISE

These words alone do not establish the cause, severity or treatment of the inflammation.

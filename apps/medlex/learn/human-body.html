Decode the precise version

Every highlighted word is a coordinate.

Four coordinates turn an ambiguous note into a reproducible, searchable, legally defensible clinical fact. That is what this chapter gives you: a coordinate system for the human body.

“The arm” is not a location

In medical language the arm ( ) is only the shoulder-to-elbow segment. The forearm, wrist and hand are different regions — each with its own name.

One language, every specialty

Surgeons, radiographers, nurses, coders and pathologists share the same positional vocabulary — Terminologia Anatomica is its ISO-style standard (FIPAT/IFAA, 2nd ed., 2019).

Your learning target

By the end of this deck you will describe any point in the body the way a GPS describes a city — without ambiguity.

06 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Four traps that cost marks — and mistakes

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

TRAP 01

Your right ≠ the patient's right

Stand at the foot of the bed facing the patient and their right hand is on your left — like a mirror. Laterality is always written from the patient's perspective .

TRAP 02

Posture never changes the terms

Supine, prone or standing on your head — “anterior” still means the front of the body. The anatomical position is a conceptual reference , not a requirement for the patient.

TRAP 03

“Arm” and “leg” are segments, not limbs

Brachium = shoulder→elbow. Antebrachium = elbow→wrist. Crus (leg) = knee→ankle. The whole appendages are the upper and lower limbs .

TRAP 04

Palms forward is deliberate

Turning the palms forward swings the thumbs laterally , exposing the dorsum (back) of the forearm. That single rotation is why “medial border of the hand” is unambiguous.

Body planes — how anatomy becomes an image

Sagittal

Divides the body into right and left portions. A median plane passes through the midline; parasagittal planes are parallel and off the midline.

02

Frontal / coronal

Divides anterior and posterior portions.

03

Divides superior and inferior portions. Imaging can be acquired or reconstructed in multiple planes.

Oblique

Passes through at an angle to the standard planes.

10 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Body regions I — head, neck & trunk

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

Every region name ends in a Latin root you can reuse for life

Head — cephalic

The head is divided into the cranium (skull) and the face . Sub-regions: frontal (forehead), orbital (eye), buccal (cheek), nasal, oral, mental (chin), auricular (ear), occipital (back of head).

Word-root decoder

-ac / -ic = “pertaining to” · gluteal ← Gk. (buttock) · umbilical ← L. (navel) · sacral ← L. (sacred bone). Master roots and you can decode regions you were never taught.

Head — cephalic

CAPUT · CEPHALIC REGION The head divides into the cranium (skull) and the face . Sub-regions: frontal (forehead), orbital (eye), buccal (cheek), nasal, oral, mental (chin), auricular (ear), occipital (back of head). Root: Gk. kephalē = head → cephalic · L. frons = forehead → frontal

Neck — cervical

COLLUM · CERVICAL REGION The neck connects head and trunk and houses the airway, oesophagus, major vessels and the cervical spine (C1–C7). “Cervical” is used for the neck and the cervix of the uterus — context decides. Root: L. cervix = neck → cervical · Gk. trachēlos = windpipe area

Chest — thoracic

THORAX · THORACIC REGION Bounded by the ribs and sternum. Sub-regions include sternal (midline), pectoral (chest wall) and mammary (breast). Contains the heart, lungs and great vessels. Root: Gk. thōrax = chest/armour → thoracic · L. sternon = breastbone

Abdomen — abdominal

ABDOMEN · ABDOMINAL REGION Between thorax and pelvis — no bony wall in front. Sub-regions: umbilical (navel), lumbar (flank). Its nine-region grid deserves its own slide… and gets one. Root: L. abdomen = belly · umbilicus = navel → umbilical

Pelvis — pelvic

PELVIS · PELVIC REGION The basin between the hip bones. Contains bladder, rectum and reproductive organs. Also called the hypogastric or pubic region in surface anatomy. Root: L. pelvis = basin → pelvic · Gk. hypogastrium = below the belly

Armpit — axillary

AXILLA · AXILLARY REGION The pyramidal space beneath the shoulder — a gateway for the brachial plexus and axillary vessels, and a key lymph-node station (breast cancer staging). Root: L. axilla = armpit → axillary

Lower back — lumbar

LUMBUS · LUMBAR REGION The flank area between ribs and pelvis, overlying the lumbar spine and kidneys. “Lumbar puncture” enters the spinal canal here — below the cord's end. Root: L. lumbus = loin → lumbar

Groin — inguinal

INGUEN · INGUINAL REGION The crease between trunk and thigh. Home of the inguinal ligament , hernia gates and deep lymph nodes. Also called the iliac region in the 9-region grid. Root: L. inguen = groin → inguinal · ilia = flank bones → iliac

11 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Body regions II — upper & lower limbs

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

Mirror zones exist on the left limb — names are identical

Arm — brachial

The upper limb is a chain of named segments: acromial (shoulder) → brachial (arm) → antecubital (front of elbow) → antebrachial (forearm) → carpal (wrist) → metacarpal/digital (hand & fingers).

Two surfaces you must name correctly

Hand: palmar (anterior, palm) vs dorsum of hand (posterior). Foot: plantar (sole) vs dorsum of foot (top). In the anatomical position the thumb sits laterally and the forearm's dorsum faces posteriorly.

Shoulder — acromial / deltoid

ACROMION · SHOULDER The shoulder point ( acromion ) and the rounded deltoid region. Injection site for many vaccines. Root: Gk. akromion = shoulder point → acromial · deltoid = triangle-shaped

Arm — brachial

BRACHIUM · UPPER ARM The segment between shoulder and elbow. Note the trap: everyday language calls the whole limb “the arm” — anatomy does not. Root: L. brachium = arm → brachial

Elbow pit — antecubital

ANTE CUBITUM · FRONT OF ELBOW The depression in front of the elbow — the classic site for venepuncture and the median cubital vein. Root: L. ante = before + cubitus = elbow → antecubital

Forearm — antebrachial

ANTEBRACHIUM · FOREARM Between elbow and wrist — radius and ulna. Two surfaces: palmar (anterior) and dorsal (posterior). Root: L. antebrachium = before the arm → forearm

Wrist — carpal

CARPUS · WRIST The eight carpal bones. “Carpal tunnel syndrome” names the narrow passageway here for the median nerve. Root: Gk. karpos = wrist → carpal

Hand — manual / metacarpal

MANUS · HAND Palm ( palma ), back of hand ( dorsum ), and the five metacarpals. The hand is a precision instrument — its regions are named bone by bone. Root: L. manus = hand → manual · Gk. metakarpion = beyond the wrist

Digits — digital / phalangeal

DIGITI · FINGERS / TOES Digits I–V (thumb to little finger). Each digit has phalanges ; the thumb has two, the others three. Root: L. digitus = finger/toe → digital · Gk. phalanx = line of soldiers

Hip — coxal

COXA · HIP The hip region over the hip joint. “Coxalgia” is hip pain; the coxal bone is the hip bone (ilium + ischium + pubis). Root: L. coxa = hip → coxal

Thigh — femoral

FEMUR · THIGH Between hip and knee, containing the femur — the body's longest bone. The femoral triangle is a key vascular landmark. Root: L. femur = thigh bone → femoral

Knee — patellar

PATELLA · KNEE The knee region, named for the patella (kneecap). The patellar reflex is tested here (L3–L4). Root: L. patella = small pan → patellar

Leg — crural

CRUS · LEG Strictly the knee-to-ankle segment (tibia &amp; fibula). Another trap: “leg” in anatomy does not mean the whole lower limb. Root: L. crus = leg → crural

Ankle — tarsal

TARSUS · ANKLE The seven tarsal bones including the calcaneus (heel) and talus. “Tarsal” also names the eyelid plate — context decides. Root: Gk. tarsos = flat of the foot → tarsal

Foot — pedal / plantar / dorsum

PES · FOOT The foot has two named surfaces: plantar (sole) and dorsum (top). Pedal pulses are checked at the dorsum. Root: L. pes, pedis = foot → pedal · planta = sole → plantar

Abdominal regions & quadrants — two maps, one belly

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

Click any tile · switch maps with the toggle above

Right hypochondriac region

“Hypochondriac” = (below) + (cartilage) — beneath the costal cartilages. Here you find the right lobe of the liver , gallbladder , part of the right kidney and hepatic flexure of the colon.

⚑ Clinical: RUQ pain → think biliary colic, hepatitis, right basal pneumonia.

Why 9 regions?

Precision for physical examination, anatomy teaching and surgical anatomy — localises deeper structures.

Why 4 quadrants?

Speed — the everyday language of ward rounds: “pain in the RLQ” instantly raises appendicitis.

Landmarks that anchor the grid

Umbilicus (T10 dermatome) is the quadrant origin. The 9-region grid uses the transpyloric plane (L1), subcostal plane , intertubercular plane and the two midclavicular lines .

rhyp

REGIO HYPOCHONDRIACA DEXTRA · Right hypochondriac region · “Hypochondriac” = <i>hypo</i> (below) + <i>chondros</i> (cartilage) — beneath the costal cartilages. Here live the <b>right lobe of the liver</b>, <b>gallbladder</b>, part of the <b>right kidney</b> and the hepatic flexure of the colon. · ⚑ Clinical: RUQ pain → think biliary colic, hepatitis, right basal pneumonia.

epi

REGIO EPIGASTRICA · Epigastric region · Above the stomach — contains most of the <b>stomach</b>, <b>left liver lobe</b>, <b>pancreas</b>, duodenum and the aorta's origin. “Epigastric” = <i>upon the stomach</i>. · ⚑ Clinical: epigastric pain after meals suggests peptic ulcer disease or pancreatitis.

lhyp

REGIO HYPOCHONDRIACA SINISTRA · Left hypochondriac region · Beneath the left costal margin — <b>spleen</b>, stomach fundus, tail of pancreas, splenic flexure of the colon, left kidney. · ⚑ Clinical: left upper pain + trauma → always consider splenic injury.

rlum

REGIO LUMBARIS DEXTRA · Right lumbar (flank) region · The right flank — <b>ascending colon</b>, right kidney, duodenum. “Lumbar” = of the loin. · ⚑ Clinical: flank pain radiating to the groin = classic renal colic.

umb

REGIO UMBILICALIS · Umbilical region · Centred on the <b>umbilicus</b> — small intestine coils, transverse colon, abdominal aorta &amp; IVC deep behind them. · ⚑ Clinical: periumbilical pain that migrates right-iliac = early appendicitis (visceral → parietal shift).

llum

REGIO LUMBARIS SINISTRA · Left lumbar (flank) region · The left flank — <b>descending colon</b>, left kidney, spleen's lower reach. · ⚑ Clinical: left flank tenderness — kidney, colon or musculoskeletal? Use the region map to reason.

ring

REGIO INGUINALIS DEXTRA · Right inguinal (iliac) region · The right groin triangle — <b>caecum</b>, <b>appendix</b>, right ureter, inguinal lymph nodes. · ⚑ Clinical: RLQ tenderness at McBurney's point = appendicitis until proven otherwise.

hyp

REGIO HYPOGASTRICA · Hypogastric (suprapubic) region · Below the navel — <b>urinary bladder</b>, sigmoid colon, uterus (when present), pelvic vessels. · ⚑ Clinical: suprapubic distension &amp; dullness = think urinary retention.

ling

REGIO INGUINALIS SINISTRA · Left inguinal (iliac) region · The left groin — <b>sigmoid colon</b>, left ureter, inguinal ring. · ⚑ Clinical: LLQ pain + altered bowel habit → diverticular disease is common here.

ruq

QUADRANS SUPERIOR DEXTER · Right upper quadrant (RUQ) · Liver &amp; gallbladder, right kidney, hepatic flexure, part of the duodenum and head of pancreas behind. · ⚑ Clinical: the everyday ward shortcut of the right hypochondriac + epigastric regions.

luq

QUADRANS SUPERIOR SINISTER · Left upper quadrant (LUQ) · Stomach, spleen, left kidney, splenic flexure, body/tail of pancreas. · ⚑ Clinical: LUQ tenderness — stomach, spleen, pancreas or splenic flexure.

rlq

QUADRANS INFERIOR DEXTER · Right lower quadrant (RLQ) · Appendix, caecum, right ovary/tube &amp; ureter, right inguinal nodes. · ⚑ Clinical: the appendicitis quadrant — also ectopic pregnancy and inguinal hernia.

llq

QUADRANS INFERIOR SINISTER · Left lower quadrant (LLQ) · Sigmoid colon, left ovary/tube &amp; ureter, left inguinal region. · ⚑ Clinical: LLQ pain — diverticulitis is the signature diagnosis.

Body cavities & serous membranes

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

Cavity map · schematic sagittal organisation

Hover a cavity layer card to explore · the dorsal cavity houses the CNS

Cranial + Vertebral (spinal) cavities

Continuous spaces protecting the brain and spinal cord , lined by meninges and cushioned by CSF.

Thoracic cavity

Contains two pleural cavities (lungs) and the pericardial cavity (heart); the space between is the mediastinum .

Abdominopelvic cavity

Abdominal (digestive organs, kidneys, spleen) above; pelvic (bladder, rectum, reproductive organs) below — no physical wall between them.

Serous membranes — the double-layer trick

Parietal layer lines the wall, visceral layer coats the organ, with lubricating serous fluid between (pleura · pericardium · peritoneum). Inflammation lets the two layers rub — producing the friction rub you can hear with a stethoscope.

16 / EXPLORE & APPLY

Memory anchors — built to stick

Explore the detail panels, retrieve the meaning, then explain the distinction.

P PALMS

Forward = thumbs lateral

The anatomical position exists so that one detail — thumbs pointing away from the midline — is never ambiguous.

M MIDLINE

Medial = toward the middle

Find the midline first. Medial/lateral, right/left and ipsilateral/contralateral all resolve instantly.

S SIDE-TO-SIDE

Sagittal splits the sides

“Sagittal = Side-to-side split.” Frontal is the face-off (front/back); transverse takes the top off.

P PROXIMITY

Proximal = proximity

Proximal means proximity to the limb's attachment point. Distal has travelled far from it.

📦 The 10-second summary

The anatomical position is the zero point. Directional terms are the axes. Planes are the cuts. Regions are the addresses. Cavities & membranes are the rooms and their linings. Combine them — measurement + direction + region + structure — and you speak medicine.

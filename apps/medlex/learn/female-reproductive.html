Ovaries & uterine tubes

Ovaries contain oocytes and produce hormones. Uterine tubes transport the oocyte and early embryo.

Uterus & cervix

The cervix is the lower part of the uterus. It opens into the vagina.

Vagina & vulva

The vagina is an internal canal. The vulva comprises the external genital structures.

Prefixes & suffixes change the meaning

a- PREFIX · WITHOUT men/o COMBINING FORM · MENSTRUATION -rrhoea SUFFIX · FLOW

-itis / -algia

Inflammation / pain: cervicitis, mastalgia.

-ectomy / -scopy

Surgical removal / visual examination: salpingectomy, hysteroscopy.

dys- / oligo-

Painful, difficult or abnormal / few or scanty: dysmenorrhoea, oligomenorrhoea.

Within / around: endometrium, perimetrium.

10 / Connect language with function

Hormones & life-stage terms

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

FSH & LH

Follicle-stimulating hormone supports follicular development. A mid-cycle LH surge triggers ovulation.

Oestrogen & progesterone

Oestrogen supports endometrial proliferation. After ovulation, corpus-luteum progesterone supports secretory changes.

Menarche

The first menstrual period. This differs from menopause.

Menopause & perimenopause

Natural menopause is recognised after 12 months without menstruation when no other cause explains it. Perimenopause is the transition around menopause.

Amenorrhoea means absent menstrual periods. It is a finding with possible physiological and pathological causes, rather than one diagnosis.

12 / Localise the disease

Similar names, different conditions

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Endometriosis

Tissue similar to endometrium occurs outside the uterus. It can cause pain and impaired fertility.

Adenomyosis

Endometrial glands and stroma occur within the myometrium.

Leiomyoma / fibroid

A benign smooth-muscle tumour of the uterus.

Endometrial polyp

A localised growth arising from the endometrium.

Endometriosis differs from endometritis, which means inflammation of the endometrium. The ending matters, and the full clinical definition matters too.

13 / Apply anatomical precision

Ovarian & inflammatory terms

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Polycystic ovary syndrome · PCOS

A hormonal and metabolic syndrome associated with ovulatory dysfunction and/or androgen excess. Multiple ovarian follicles may be present.

Ovarian cyst

A fluid-filled or partly fluid-filled ovarian structure. A cyst alone does not establish PCOS.

Cervicitis / vaginitis

Inflammation of the cervix / vagina, respectively.

Pelvic inflammatory disease · PID

Infection and inflammation involving the upper reproductive tract, often including the uterus and uterine tubes.

Salpingitis = inflammation of a uterine tube. PID can follow sexually transmitted and other infections. The term alone does not identify an organism.

14 / Investigations & abbreviations

Screening is different from diagnosis

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

HPV test / Pap test

HPV testing detects high-risk viral infection. Cervical cytology examines cells for abnormalities.

Colposcopy & biopsy

Colposcopy magnifies the cervix and related areas. Biopsy samples tissue for pathological examination.

TVUS / pelvic ultrasound

Transvaginal ultrasonography uses a vaginal probe. Transabdominal imaging is another approach.

Hysteroscopy / HSG

Hysteroscopy views the uterine cavity. Hysterosalpingography uses contrast X-ray imaging to assess the cavity and tubal patency.

An abnormal screening result does not equal cancer. A Pap test is not an ovarian cancer screening test. Test choice and screening intervals depend on current local guidance and clinical history.

15 / Surgical precision

Procedures: what is removed?

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Total / subtotal hysterectomy

Total removes the uterus and cervix. Subtotal, or supracervical, retains the cervix.

Salpingectomy / oophorectomy

Removal of a uterine tube / ovary. Laterality must be specified.

Salpingo-oophorectomy

Removal of the tube and ovary on the stated side or sides.

Myomectomy / endometrial ablation

Myomectomy removes fibroids. Ablation destroys or removes endometrial tissue.

Bilateral means both sides. Unilateral means one side. Hysterectomy does not automatically mean that the ovaries were removed.

16 / Name the approach

Fertility & contraception language

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Ovulation / anovulation

Release of an oocyte from an ovary / absence of ovulation.

IVF / IUI

In vitro fertilisation involves fertilisation outside the body. Intrauterine insemination places prepared sperm in the uterus.

IUD / IUS

An intrauterine device or system sits within the uterine cavity. Terminology varies by region and product.

Tubal occlusion / salpingectomy

Tubal occlusion blocks the uterine tubes. Salpingectomy removes a tube or tubes.

Contraception aims to prevent pregnancy. Infertility describes difficulty achieving pregnancy. A procedure’s name does not by itself determine its purpose or guarantee an outcome.

19 / Consolidation & exit task

The distinctions to retain

Retrieve five terms tomorrow without looking, then review the meanings you missed.

Anatomy

Vulva is external. Vagina is an internal canal. Cervix is part of the uterus.

Symptoms

Amenorrhoea: absent periods. Dysmenorrhoea: painful periods. Describe bleeding patterns clearly.

Disorders

Endometriosis, adenomyosis and endometritis describe different conditions.

Procedures

Hysterectomy concerns the uterus. Oophorectomy concerns the ovary. Salpingectomy concerns the tube.

Exit task: decode “bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy”, explain “dysmenorrhoea”, and compare an HPV test with a Pap test.

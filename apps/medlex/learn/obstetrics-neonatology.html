03 / ANATOMY EXPLORER

Name the structures before the conditions

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Original schematic · not to scale · use slider for 3D tilt

Uterus & cervix

The uterus supports the pregnancy; the cervix is its lower, narrow portion, containing a canal that opens into the vagina. Roots: hyster/o or metr/o (uterus), cervic/o (cervix).

Amnion & chorion

Fetal membranes surround the pregnancy. Amni/o relates to the amnion or amniotic fluid; chori/o relates to the chorion.

Placenta & umbilical cord

The placenta supports exchange between maternal and fetal circulations. The cord connects fetus and placenta; maternal and fetal blood do not normally mix directly.

06 / DATING & TERM CATEGORIES

Gestational age is a clock, not a size

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Gestational age describes elapsed time, not birth weight. This teaching scale uses term categories; clinical dating requires the appropriate assessment.

Gestational age (GA) is usually counted from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), with ultrasound used to establish or refine dating. EDD = estimated due date.

35+2 means 35 completed weeks and 2 days. It does not mean 35.2 decimal weeks.

07 / OBSTETRIC HISTORY

Gravidity counts pregnancies; parity counts events

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

G · gravidity

Total pregnancies, including the current one. A twin pregnancy counts once.

P · parity

Pregnancies reaching the locally defined gestational threshold, regardless of the number of infants or survival.

TPAL

Term births · Preterm births · Abortions / losses below the threshold · Living children.

Currently pregnant; one previous twin birth at 38 weeks; both children living; no losses.
Translate the example

G2 P1; G2 T1 P0 A0 L2 in TPAL notation. The two uses of P differ: total parity versus the preterm category.

Many US systems use ≥20 weeks for parity. Definitions and notation vary; use the institution’s standard. The A category includes spontaneous and induced losses.

08 / ANTENATAL TESTS

Screening estimates chance; diagnosis tests for a condition

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Screening

“How likely?”

Cell-free DNA (cfDNA, often called NIPT) screens for selected chromosomal conditions. A positive screen is not a diagnosis.

Diagnostic testing

“Is it present?”

CVS samples chorionic villi. Amniocentesis obtains amniotic fluid. Laboratory analysis can diagnose the conditions tested.

What does ultrasonography add?

Sound-wave imaging helps assess dating, anatomy and growth. It does not exclude every genetic or structural condition.

Updated ACOG screening advisory: January 2026. Testing choices follow informed counselling; no test detects every condition.

09 / LABOUR & DELIVERY

Labour changes the cervix, then delivers baby and placenta

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

1

First stage

Contractions accompany cervical effacement (thinning) and dilation (opening) to full dilation.

2

Second stage

Full dilation to birth of the baby. Full cervical dilation is approximately 10 cm.

3

Third stage

Birth of the baby to delivery of the placenta.

Induction

Starting labour before it begins spontaneously.

Augmentation

Strengthening labour that has already begun.

Caesarean birth

Birth through incisions in the abdomen and uterus; also spelled cesarean.

11 / OBSTETRIC CONDITIONS

Complication terms name different problems

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Preeclampsia

A pregnancy-related hypertensive disorder, usually after 20 weeks, with proteinuria and/or other organ dysfunction; it can also occur postpartum.

Eclampsia

Seizures associated with preeclampsia, after other causes are considered.

Placenta previa

Placenta overlies the internal cervical opening.

Placental abruption

Premature separation of a normally implanted placenta before birth.

PPH · postpartum haemorrhage

Excessive bleeding after birth; diagnostic criteria depend on the clinical guideline.

Uterine atony

Insufficient uterine contraction after birth; an important cause of PPH.

Terminology only. WHO consolidated PPH guidance (2025) and its 2026 implementation guide inform current clinical practice.

13 / NEONATAL CLASSIFICATION

A newborn has an age and a size

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Neonatal period = birth through the first 28 days of life (0–27 completed days).

Preterm

Born alive before 37 completed gestational weeks. This describes timing.

Low birth weight · LBW

Birth weight <2,500 g, irrespective of gestational age. This describes absolute weight.

Small for gestational age · SGA

Birth weight below the 10th percentile for gestational age and sex on the chosen reference chart.

Can a baby be both preterm and low birth weight?

Yes. A baby born at 35+2 weeks weighing 2,200 g is preterm and LBW. SGA cannot be assigned without the appropriate growth chart.

VLBW = <1,500 g · ELBW = <1,000 g. SGA is not synonymous with fetal growth restriction (FGR).

14 / ASSESSMENT VOCABULARY

Apgar describes condition after birth

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

Recorded at 1 and 5 minutes; repeated when indicated. Five components receive 0, 1 or 2 points each, for a total of 0–10.

A

Appearance

Color

P

Pulse

Heart rate

G

Grimace

Reflex response

A

Activity

Muscle tone

R

Respiration

Breathing effort

Check the arithmetic: 1 + 2 + 1 + 2 + 2

Total = 8. This example practises the scoring vocabulary; a score alone does not establish the cause of a newborn’s condition.

Do not wait for an Apgar score to begin indicated resuscitation. It does not independently diagnose asphyxia or predict an individual child’s long-term outcome.

16 / CASE LAB

Translate a complete handover

Explore the detail panels. Retrieve the meaning. Explain the distinction.

“G2P1 at 35+2 weeks with confirmed PPROM. Following birth, a 2,200 g neonate is admitted to NICU for respiratory distress.”
1 · Explain the obstetric history

Second pregnancy, with one previous pregnancy reaching the local parity threshold. G2P1 does not specify the number of living children.

2 · Classify the newborn

Preterm and low birth weight. To determine whether the newborn is SGA, use an appropriate gestational-age growth chart.

3 · Avoid an unsupported conclusion

Respiratory distress describes a problem. It does not by itself confirm respiratory distress syndrome or any other specific cause.

Hypothetical language exercise. The history notation refers to the record before this birth; parity is updated afterwards.

Foundations

Language shapes clinical meaning

Foundations / Describe the experience and preserve the person.

01

Symptom ≠ diagnosis

Anhedonia is a symptom. A diagnosis requires a fuller assessment, including context and functioning.

02

Person-first and preference-aware

Use “person with schizophrenia” or “person with a substance use disorder”. Respect individual identity and language preferences.

03

Frameworks provide a shared vocabulary

WHO’s ICD-11 and APA’s DSM-5-TR classify disorders. Their terminology and criteria are not identical.

Record what the person says, what you observe, and what remains uncertain. Avoid using a diagnostic label as an adjective for everyday behaviour.

Assessment

A map of the mental status examination

Assessment / The MSE describes a person’s current presentation.

01 Appearance & behaviour

Observable grooming, engagement, and motor activity. Describe behaviour without judging character.

02 Speech

Rate, volume, quantity, and fluency. “Pressured” describes speech that is unusually driven and difficult to interrupt.

03 Mood & affect

Mood is the person’s reported emotional experience. Affect is the emotional expression observed during the encounter.

04 Thought

Process: how ideas connect. Content: what the person is thinking about.

05 Perception

Experiences such as hallucinations or misinterpretation of a real stimulus.

06 Cognition, insight & judgement

Attention, orientation, and memory; understanding of one’s situation; and decision-making in context.

The MSE is a snapshot. A complete assessment also uses history, physical health, substances, culture, communication needs, and collateral information.

Assessment

Mood and affect answer different questions

Assessment / A reported experience and an observed expression can differ.

REPORTED EXPERIENCE

Mood

“I feel anxious most of the day.”

Use the person’s own words when helpful.

OBSERVED EXPRESSION

Affect

“Emotional expression appears constricted.”

Describe range, intensity, reactivity, and context.

ILLUSTRATION, NOT AN ASSESSMENT TOOL

A drawing cannot establish someone’s mood or diagnosis.

Constricted : limited range. Blunted : reduced intensity. Flat : little observable expression. These are descriptions, not diagnoses.

Clinical terms

Depression, mania, and hypomania

Clinical terms / Clinical episodes involve a pattern over time.

Term Core meaning Distinction to remember
Depressive episode Sustained depressed mood or loss of interest, with associated symptoms and impairment. Everyday sadness alone does not establish a depressive disorder.
Mania Markedly elevated, expansive, or irritable mood with increased energy/activity and other changes. May cause major disruption, require hospital care, or include psychosis.
Hypomania A distinct change in mood and energy that is less severe than mania. No psychosis or marked functional impairment; a clear change from baseline remains important.
Bipolar I includes at least one manic episode. Bipolar II includes hypomanic and major depressive episodes, with no history of a manic episode.

Clinical terms

Anxiety, fear, and panic

Clinical terms / A panic attack can occur in more than one condition.

01

Anxiety

Apprehension or worry, often about anticipated threat. It can occur as an ordinary experience or a clinical symptom.

02

Generalized anxiety disorder · GAD

Persistent, difficult-to-control worry across everyday areas, with other symptoms and interference in life.

03

Panic attack

An abrupt surge of intense fear or discomfort with psychological and physical symptoms.

04

Panic disorder

Recurrent unexpected panic attacks with ongoing concern or related behavioural change.

Clinical terms

Obsessions, compulsions, and trauma-related terms

Clinical terms / The words describe different experiences.

OCD VOCABULARY

Obsession / compulsion

Obsession: recurrent intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images, or urges.

Compulsion: repetitive behaviour or a mental act, often performed in response to an obsession.

A mental act can be a compulsion

Repeating words silently or counting can be compulsive, even when no outward ritual is visible.

TRAUMA VOCABULARY

PTSD / dissociation

PTSD: post-traumatic stress disorder, involving a persistent pattern after traumatic exposure.

Dissociation: disruption in the usual integration of awareness, memory, identity, or perception.

Two related descriptions

Depersonalisation: feeling detached from oneself. Derealisation: surroundings feel unreal. These are distinct from hallucinations.

OCD is more than a preference for order. Exposure to trauma alone does not establish PTSD. Exact diagnostic requirements differ across classification systems.

Clinical terms

Cognition and substance-related vocabulary

Clinical terms / Similar presentations can have different causes.

Delirium

An acute disturbance of attention and awareness, typically fluctuating over hours or days. It requires prompt medical evaluation.

Dementia

An acquired decline in cognitive abilities that interferes with independent daily functioning. Delirium can occur on top of dementia.

Intoxication / withdrawal

Intoxication: substance-related effects after use. Withdrawal: symptoms after reduction or cessation following repeated use.

Tolerance / physical dependence

Tolerance: reduced response to the same dose. Physical dependence: adaptation associated with withdrawal after reduction or stopping.

Substance use disorder · SUD

A clinically significant pattern of substance use associated with impairment or distress. Physical dependence alone does not establish it.

Recovery / remission

Recovery concerns a person’s life, goals, and functioning. Remission describes reduced or absent symptoms according to the relevant disorder’s definition.

A rapid change in attention should not be dismissed as “just psychiatric”. Describe the change, timeline, and context.

Assessment

Screening and safety language require precision

Assessment / A score supports an assessment; it cannot replace one.

Term Meaning Documentation point
MSE Mental status examination A structured description of the current presentation.
PHQ-9 / GAD-7 Patient Health Questionnaire-9 / Generalized Anxiety Disorder-7 Questionnaires used to assess symptom burden and support screening.
Suicidal ideation A wish to be dead or thoughts of ending one’s life Document the report and assess further; thoughts, intent, and plans are distinct.
Self-harm Intentional self-poisoning or injury, irrespective of apparent purpose, in NICE guidance Do not assume the presence or absence of suicidal intent.
NICE advises against using risk scales to predict future suicide or repetition of self-harm. Clinical assessment addresses needs, context, and immediate safety.

Care vocabulary

Treatment words describe an approach

Care vocabulary / Expand the abbreviation before explaining its role.

Psychotherapy / CBT

Psychotherapy uses psychological methods. Cognitive behavioural therapy examines relationships among thoughts, feelings, and behaviour.

ERP

Exposure and response prevention: a form of CBT involving exposure to feared situations while reducing compulsive responses.

SSRI / SNRI

Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor / serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor. Medication classes used for several conditions.

Antipsychotic / mood stabiliser

Treatment classes or clinical groupings. A medicine’s name alone does not reveal the person’s diagnosis.

ECT

Electroconvulsive therapy: a procedure using electrical stimulation to induce a controlled seizure under anaesthesia.

rTMS

Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation: repeated magnetic pulses stimulate targeted brain regions.

Care is individualised and often combines approaches. Shared decision-making considers goals, preferences, benefits, and adverse effects.

Application

The original note is now readable

Application / Explain each phrase without adding a diagnosis.

The person describes reduced pleasure and difficulty sleeping. These are reported symptoms.

“Affect constricted”

The clinician observes a limited range of emotional expression during the encounter.

“Thought process linear”

The person’s ideas connect in a logical sequence during the interview.

What is a root?

A root carries a word’s core meaning. It may name a body part, tissue, substance or process. Add a combining vowel, often o, and you have a combining form such as cardi/o.

Find the core. Ask: what is this word about?

✦

Why learn roots?

One root can help you recognise a family of words. Meet cardi- in cardiology, then notice it again in cardiomyopathy. You build connections that make unfamiliar terms easier to explore.

A helpful clue—not a substitute for the complete meaning.

What is a ROOT?

Think of a medical word as a tree. The root is the trunk — it carries the core meaning, usually a body part, substance or process. Once you can spot the root, long words stop looking scary and start telling you their story.

Most medical words are built like this: a prefix at the start adjusts the meaning, the ROOT gives the main meaning, and a suffix at the end often says what kind of condition or procedure it is. Tap each block — can you spot the root?

🔗 The magic joining “o” — and root twins

Roots often put on a combining vowel, usually -o-, to connect smoothly to the next part. Flip the switch and hear/see why cardio- is called a combining form (root + o).

cardiroot = heart+ + -logystudy of
cardi + logy
😬 Without the “o”, the word feels bumpy and hard to say. Flip the switch ON to make the correct combining form.
✂️ The “o” often drops before a vowel

gastr + -itis → gastritis (no “o” needed). But gastr + o + -scopy → gastroscopy.

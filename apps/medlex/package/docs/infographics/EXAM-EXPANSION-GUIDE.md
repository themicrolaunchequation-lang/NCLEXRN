Sixteen new PNG posters extend the library from 40 to 56. The selection focuses on common sources of terminology error: near-identical word forms, over-interpreting a suffix, clinical categories that sound interchangeable, spatial reference frames, and recall of exact word-part meanings. ## How to teach and examine with the posters

1. Ask the learner to state the meaning before opening the study text.
2. Ask for the reference point, tissue, modifier or clinical context that makes the answer precise.
3. Present the tempting alternative and ask why it is wrong.
4. Use the separate recall answer to give feedback. Ask the learner to correct their explanation in their own words.
5. Revisit the distinction in a different example after a gap. Track actual practice separately from simply viewing a poster.

For body planes, have learners name the portions separated. For positions, identify the patient’s side and the side that is down. For memory cues, always finish with the literal meaning. The posters do not introduce new scored question modes or automatically award practice credit.

### 41 · Similar roots, different tissues

**Group:** Common pitfalls. **Related module:** ch1.

**Teaching focus:** Distinguish similar forms and explain why the tempting alternative is wrong.

**Exam distinction:** Read every letter. A similar sound does not establish the same meaning.

**Recall:** Which root can refer to either marrow or spinal cord?

**Answer:** myel/o; verify the complete term and context.

**Checking references:**

### 43 · What an ending cannot tell you

**Group:** Common pitfalls. **Related module:** suffixes.

**Teaching focus:** Distinguish similar forms and explain why the tempting alternative is wrong.

**Exam distinction:** Word parts offer clues. They do not establish cause, severity or a diagnosis.

**Recall:** Does an -itis ending prove infection?

**Answer:** No. It indicates inflammation; the cause must be established separately.

### 44 · Abbreviations: clarify, do not guess

**Group:** Common pitfalls. **Related module:** ch1.

**Teaching focus:** Distinguish similar forms and explain why the tempting alternative is wrong.

**Exam distinction:** These are error-prone examples, not abbreviations to copy into clinical instructions.

**Recall:** What should you do when an abbreviation has two plausible meanings?

**Answer:** Clarify the intended meaning and write it in full; do not choose by guesswork.

### 45 · Sign, symptom, syndrome, diagnosis

**Group:** Clinical concepts. **Related module:** ch1.

**Teaching focus:** Select the term that matches the evidence and preserve its limits.

**Exam distinction:** A finding is evidence. A diagnosis is an interpretation supported by evidence.

**Recall:** Is a person reporting nausea describing a sign or a symptom?

**Answer:** A symptom.

### 46 · Time course is not severity

**Group:** Clinical concepts. **Related module:** ch19.

**Teaching focus:** Select the term that matches the evidence and preserve its limits.

**Exam distinction:** Acute does not simply mean severe. Chronic does not simply mean mild. Definitions of duration vary by condition.

**Recall:** Does chronic tell you that a condition is mild?

**Answer:** No. It describes persistence, not severity.

### 48 · Infection is not the same as colonization

## Production and review

Every poster includes MedLex, Healthcare Crown Academy and the crest branding. All sixteen additions are illustrated PNG posters made with the built-in image-generation tool in the existing watercolor teaching style. Five earlier direction/position schematics were replaced with these illustrated versions. These are conceptual learning illustrations; the companion definitions are the reference. The app uses optimized WebPs and preserves text transcripts, checking references and recall answers.

Text, distinction wording, labels, spatial relationships and file integrity received development review. Independent medical-editor review remains pending. Automated DOM, storage, export and offline checks cannot establish native browser layout, rendered contrast, real touch/fullscreen behavior or screen-reader behavior.
